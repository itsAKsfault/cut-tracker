Daily Core Routine

Core strength built from the bottom up: breathing and deep abs first, then hips and glutes, then holding your trunk still against force. Low effort per session, so it's safe to do daily. It's also SI-friendly, so your friend can do it with you.

Show this to your doctor or a physical therapist.

"Work on core from the bottom up" can mean a few different things: pelvic floor and deep abs, hips and glutes, or general trunk stability. This routine covers all three in that order, but I'm interpreting your doctor's words, not repeating their prescription. Ask whether a PT referral makes sense. Stop any move that causes sharp, shooting, or radiating pain, or pain that's worse the next morning.

Why this order

Bottom up, in four layers

LayerWhat it trainsMoves
1 · Breath and braceDiaphragm, pelvic floor, deep abs working together. The base everything else sits on.90/90 breathing, pelvic tilts
2 · Hips and glutesThe muscles that hold the pelvis steady under your spineGlute bridge, clamshell
3 · Anti-movementKeeping the trunk still while arms and legs move: forward, back, sideways, and against twistingDead bug, bird dog, side plank, front plank, Pallof press
4 · Carry-overUsing the brace while standing and walkingBracing on your 10k-step walks, then a suitcase carry in phase 3

Dead bug, bird dog, and side plank overlap with a well-known back-health set often called the "McGill Big 3," after spine researcher Stuart McGill. I'm naming it for context, not citing a specific study.

The routine

Do these in order, every day

Move slowly. Before every rep: breathe out, gently tighten your belly as if bracing for a light poke, and keep your ribs down. If you lose the brace, the set is over.

Fitting it into the week

Full or short version

Lifting days (Mon, Tue, Thu, Sat)

Short version, about 5 minutes, as your warm-up: moves 1, 2, 3, and 5. The workout already has core work at the end, so this primes the brace instead of tiring it out.

Pool, walk, and rest days (Wed, Fri, Sun)

Full version, all 10 moves for your phase. Morning works well, since it sets your posture for the day's steps. Can be done on a yoga mat in front of the TV.

A change I'd suggest to your workout plan

Your doctor linked your aches to a weak core. Sit-ups and the crunch puller curl the spine under load. They're not banned, but until the aches settle I'd swap them for the friend's options (dead bug and heel taps) on Days A, B, and C. Your call.

Two ways to run this

Concise vs exploratory

Concise

Start phase 1 tomorrow morning. Do the full version on non-lifting days and the short version as your lifting warm-up. Move up a phase only when every move feels controlled and pain-free.

Exploratory

Ask your doctor what "bottom up" meant and whether a PT should assess you. A PT can test which layer is actually weak: pelvic floor, glutes, or trunk endurance. Before starting, write down where your aches are and rate them 0–10 each week. That shows whether this routine is helping.
