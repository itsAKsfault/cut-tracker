Two-Person Rotation Plan

One shared session, two versions of every move. You train for muscle and fat loss; your friend trains the same muscles with SI-joint-friendly swaps. Same room, same clock, different weights.

Read before starting.

This is general fitness programming, not medical advice. I'm not citing a specific study or clinician for it. Your friend should get this plan checked by a physical therapist or doctor first, ideally one who has examined them, because "SI joint pain" is often confused with lumbar disc or hip problems and the right exercises differ. Stop any move that causes sharp, shooting, or radiating pain, numbness, or pain that is worse the next morning.

Where I'd push back

Three things to know up front

Core work won't flatten the stomach

Spot reduction doesn't work. Crunches build the muscle under the fat; the fat comes off through a calorie deficit. Your stomach and chest goals are about 80% kitchen rough estimate.

Check what the chest tissue is

Soft fat shrinks with weight loss. A firm, rubbery disc right under the nipple can be glandular gynecomastia, which no workout fixes. If you feel that, ask a doctor. It's a quick check.

Don't skip legs, even with an upper focus

Glutes are the main stabilizers of the SI joint, and big leg muscles burn the most energy. The plan keeps 1 lower day out of 4. Dropping it would hurt your friend's rehab most.

Daily core routine

Your doctor flagged core strength as your main limiting factor. Do the Daily Core Routine every day: the short 5-minute version as the warm-up on lifting days, the full 10–12 minutes on the other days.

Steps: split 10k into 2 or 3 walks. If your friend isn't at 10k yet, start at their current daily average and add about 1,000 a week common rule of thumb.

Starting weight: 15 lb on every cable exercise, week 1
  • Add 5 lb next session when you hit the top of the rep range with more reps left than the RIR target.
  • Expect the big pulls and presses (pulldown, rows, chest press, Romanian deadlift) to pass 15 lb fast, possibly by week 2. Small moves (lateral raise, face pull, curls, triceps) may stay near 15 lb for weeks. That's normal.
  • Friend: only add weight when the set is pain-free (green) and still has 3+ reps left.
  • Weights are per side. 15 lb on a two-arm move like chest press or row means 30 lb total. That's a reasonable start for the big moves.
  • Exception: on lateral raises, face pulls, and triceps, 15 lb per side may be too heavy at first, especially for your friend. Start those at 5–10 lb per side if form breaks down.
Using the adjustable bench
  • Flat: chest press, rows, box squat target.
  • Incline at 30–45°: swap in for the low-to-high fly on week 2 and 3 if you want more upper chest (incline cable press).
  • Upright at 90°: seated shoulder press, seated curls, seated pulldown. This is your friend's default seat for anything overhead.
  • Friend: avoid decline settings. Feet stay flat on the floor.
Your friend's rule for every set

The pain traffic light

0–2 / 10

Keep going as written.

3–4 / 10

Shorten the range, drop the weight, or use the listed swap. Should settle within an hour.

5+ / 10, sharp, or worse next day

Stop the move. Skip it next session and ask the PT.

This is a widely used pain-monitoring approach in rehab, not something specific to your friend. Their PT's limits override it.

General SI rules built into every swap
  • Square, two-footed stances. No lunges, split stances, or single-leg standing work early on.
  • No loaded twisting. Anti-rotation (resisting a twist) instead.
  • No sit-ups or loaded crunches. Strong hip-flexor pull can tug on the pelvis.
  • Seated with back support for overhead and pressing work when possible.
  • Brace first: gentle belly tension, ribs down, before every rep.

Recovery with what you have

Pool

  • 20–30 min on Wednesday: water walking in 3 directions, kickboard flutter kicks, arm sweeps.
  • Water takes much of the body's weight off the joints, which makes it one of the best places for your friend to move.
  • Friend: avoid breaststroke "frog" kicks. They're a commonly cited SI aggravator.
  • Massage bench: relaxed, after the swim, not before lifting.

Sauna

  • Follow the SaunaBox manual for session length and temperature. I don't know that model's specs.
  • Typical guidance is 10–20 minutes, with water before and after general guidance.
  • No alcohol beforehand. Get out if dizzy or nauseous.
  • Check with a doctor first if either of you has heart problems, blood pressure issues, or takes medication that affects either.
  • Scale weight drops after sauna are water, not fat.

Massage chair

  • Fine on rest days and after workouts.
  • Friend: lowest intensity on the low back and hips, and skip those zones during a flare.

Friday mobility (10 min)

  • Cat-cow, 10 slow reps.
  • Knee-to-chest on the back, one leg at a time, gently.
  • Doorway chest stretch, 30 s each side.
  • Supine figure-4 glute stretch, only if pain-free.
